Music Controller / Design reference / v1.0 · 9 October 2026

A music remote,
made for the hand.

One focused view. A familiar playback dock. Artwork, music and rooms given space to breathe.

This single-file design artefact consolidates the agreed interface vision, visual mockups and implementation approach for the Music Controller project. It is intended to live at docs/design/music-controller-design.html and to guide firmware work. The mockups are static specifications, not a working controller.

Canonical display: 480 × 800 pixels, portrait (3:5). Every screen below is exactly 480 × 800 CSS pixels, with no stretching. Browser zoom may scale the whole document uniformly. The document itself scrolls; the device shell does not.

1. Decisions that guide the design

  • Portrait first, subject to validating display rotation and touch mapping on the physical device.
  • Exactly three transport controls, always ordered Previous → Play/Stop → Next. There is no separate Pause button.
  • A fixed playback dock on every primary screen, including selection views and Search with its keyboard open.
  • One primary content view at a time. Lists may scroll within their allocated area.
  • Artwork-led Now Playing; calm dark styling, warm green accents and large touch targets.
  • Provider means what to listen to. Player means where to play. They have distinct selection flows.
  • Reusable LVGL components, with this HTML as the visual reference. Preserve the existing gateway and playback integration.

2. The portrait screen family

All examples share a selected player, a playing track and 45% volume to make the consistency visible. Artwork is generated locally with CSS. Provider and player entries are illustrative, not capability claims.

3. Exact screen geometry

RegionPosition / sizeResponsibility
Headerx 0, y 0 · 480 × 64View title; connection indicator and current player shortcut.
Contentx 0, y 64 · 480 × 468One active screen. Default horizontal inset 24 px; usable width 432 px.
Playback dockx 0, y 532 · 480 × 20044 px track strip, 80 px transport row, 48 px volume row; remaining 28 px for padding and separation.
Navigationx 0, y 732 · 480 × 68Playing, Queue, Browse, Search. Providers is reached from Browse; Players from the header.

Now Playing artwork is 288 × 288 px. List rows are at least 72 px tall. Controls have a minimum 48 × 48 px hit area; transport buttons are 64 × 56 px and the central button is 72 × 72 px. Search allocates approximately 200 px of its content pane to the keyboard; the results region uses the remaining height with its own scrolling. Device pixel density and finger testing may require larger targets.

4. Interaction and state specification

Trigger / stateRequired behaviour
Centre transport buttonPlaying → send Stop. Stopped or paused → send Play. Label and icon describe the action: square / Stop or triangle / Play. Keep Pause distinct internally if existing integrations require it, without exposing a fourth control.
Stop and subsequent PlayDo not assume Stop resumes at the same position. Validate gateway/provider semantics. Preserve queue context where supported; document actual restart or resume behaviour during implementation.
Command pendingShow immediate pressed/pending feedback. Prevent duplicate submissions for the same action; reconcile with authoritative gateway state. On failure, show a short error and restore available controls.
Previous / NextUse existing gateway semantics. Disable actions unsupported by the selected player or source, including stations without skip support.
VolumeApply to the selected player. Provide a 48 px touch strip around the visual slider. Throttle drag updates and send a final value on release. Show actual returned volume; disable where unsupported.
Tap player headerOpen Players and remember the previous view. Selecting a player subscribes to its state and returns to that view. Do not move music between rooms implicitly. Keep commands disabled while the switch is pending.
Tap source in BrowseOpen Providers. Selection returns to that source’s Browse view and scopes Search. Existing playback continues. Back restores the previous selection.
Queue rowTap to play that queue entry through the current integration. Highlight the authoritative active entry. Preserve stable entry identity across queue updates. Defer reorder and removal to explicit detail actions if supported.
SearchSearch the selected provider. Debounce input, cancel or ignore stale responses, and distinguish loading, no results and errors. Done hides the keyboard; an empty field shows recent searches only if implemented.
Long metadataReserve fixed heights. Wrap the main title to at most two lines; truncate list and dock labels with ellipsis. Avoid moving transport controls or continuous marquee animation.
Back navigationNested Browse, detail and selection views replace content and expose a 48 px Back target in the header. Bottom tabs return to primary views; preserve list position and search text.

Empty, radio and connection states

Idle: show a quiet artwork placeholder and “Choose something to play” with a Browse action. The dock retains its geometry; transport is disabled until a valid command context exists. Radio: use station art and programme metadata when supplied, omit duration for live streams and disable unsupported skips. Missing artwork: show the built-in placeholder immediately.

Connecting or disconnected: retain last known metadata with a visible stale/offline label, disable network commands and offer Retry in the content area. An unavailable player remains visible but disabled. Missing source credentials or provider errors use a focused message and Back action. State is never communicated by colour alone.

6. Implementation approach

The agreed implementation direction uses native LVGL on the ESP32-S3 with ESP-IDF, retaining the Atlas/Music Assistant integration. These stack and hardware details come from the design conversation and should be checked against the repository before coding. This artefact does not establish the installed LVGL version or independently verify issue #15.

  1. Validate portrait hardware. Confirm panel rotation at 480 × 800, touch-coordinate transforms, corner accuracy, enclosure orientation and memory requirements. If native rotation is unavailable, measure software rotation before accepting it.
  2. Introduce tokens and shell. Build the fixed regions and reusable components against simulated state. Match the geometry in this document before wiring screen-specific behaviour.
  3. Bind shared state and commands. Adapt existing player, track, queue, provider, volume and connectivity models. Distinguish playback state from command-pending state. Perform network work off the LVGL UI path and marshal results through the repository’s supported UI-thread mechanism.
  4. Compose the six screens. Reuse shell and dock; keep selection separate from playback actions. Implement capability-aware controls and all essential loading, empty and failure states.
  5. Add artwork safely. Resize at the gateway where existing infrastructure allows, bound decoding and caching, show placeholders immediately, and discard results belonging to an old track/player. Avoid fetching full-resolution artwork onto the device.
  6. Validate on hardware. Check touch ergonomics, playback feedback, provider behaviour, queue changes, keyboard space, memory and long-running responsiveness.

Memory and responsiveness

A single 480 × 800 RGB565 framebuffer is 768,000 bytes (~750 KiB); two are ~1.46 MiB. A 288 × 288 RGB565 artwork image is 165,888 bytes (~162 KiB), before decoder workspace and transfer buffers. The conversation assumes 8 MB PSRAM, which must be confirmed. Account separately for panel buffers, LVGL heap, images, fonts, networking and rotation buffers; do not infer usable heap from nominal PSRAM size.

Keep one active screen tree where practical; reuse the dock, use bounded list rendering and cache only a small number of resized images. Avoid blocking network calls and expensive effects in touch callbacks. HTML and LVGL share design tokens and component contracts, rather than rendering code. Use the project’s actual LVGL APIs once its version is known.

7. Acceptance checklist

  • All six views fit an actual 480 × 800 display with no shell scrolling, clipped controls or distorted artwork.
  • The dock stays at y = 532 across primary, selection and keyboard states. Previous, Play/Stop and Next preserve their order.
  • Browse and Search keep working while the dock controls the selected player.
  • Provider selection leaves the player unchanged; player selection follows that player’s queue and track without transferring playback.
  • Playing, stopped, paused, live radio, idle, unavailable, disconnected and command-failure states are usable and accurate.
  • Long track names, missing artwork, long lists and large search results stay within their regions.
  • Artwork/network updates do not block touch feedback. Establish a measured feedback target (proposed ≤100 ms for local visual response) and memory budget on the device.
  • Verify Play after Stop for the actual Sonos/gateway path, touch alignment at all four corners, volume capability and reconnect behaviour.

8. Scope and traceability

Design context: Ripper #577 remote Music Controller work and the supplied discussion of Music Controller issue #15. The attached collage establishes the current landscape UI; this document replaces its layout with the agreed portrait direction. No live repository or issue audit was performed for this consolidation.

Agreed: portrait target, three transport controls, persistent dock, separate player/provider selection and a reusable native UI. Reference choices: compact track strip, four bottom destinations, the exact tokens and geometry above. To validate: hardware rotation, installed framework versions, available providers, Stop/Play semantics and measured memory/performance. Group management, drag reordering, artwork palette extraction and dock-specific passive modes are future refinements.
