Music Controller / Design reference / v1.1 · 9 October 2026

A music remote,
made for the hand.

One focused view. Separate transport and volume docks. Artwork, music and rooms given space to breathe.

This single-file design artefact consolidates the agreed interface vision, visual mockups and implementation approach for the Music Controller project. It is intended to live at docs/design/music-controller-design.html and to guide firmware work. The mockups are static specifications, not a working controller.

Canonical display: 480 × 800 pixels, portrait (3:5). Every screen below is exactly 480 × 800 CSS pixels, with no stretching. Browser zoom may scale the whole document uniformly. The document itself scrolls; the device shell does not.

1. Decisions that guide the design

  • Portrait first, subject to validating display rotation and touch mapping on the physical device.
  • Exactly three transport controls, always ordered Previous → Play/Stop → Next. There is no separate Pause button.
  • A fixed bottom TransportDock and right-side VolumeDock on every primary screen, including selection views and Search with its keyboard open. The Now Playing timeline controls track position, not volume.
  • One primary content view at a time. Lists may scroll within their allocated area.
  • Artwork-led Now Playing; calm dark styling, warm green accents and large touch targets.
  • Provider means what to listen to. Player means where to play. They have distinct selection flows.
  • Reusable LVGL components, with this HTML as the visual reference. Preserve the existing gateway and playback integration.

2. The portrait screen family

All examples share a selected player, a playing track and 45% volume to make the consistency visible. Every view reserves the same right-side VolumeDock and full-width bottom TransportDock; Now Playing alone includes a seek timeline. Artwork is generated locally with CSS. Provider and player entries are illustrative, not capability claims.

3. Exact screen geometry

RegionPosition / sizeResponsibility
Headerx 0, y 0 · 480 × 64View title; connection indicator and current player shortcut.
Contentx 0, y 64 · 400 × 532One active screen. Horizontal inset 24 px; usable width 352 px. Lists scroll here.
VolumeDockx 400, y 64 · 80 × 532Persistent vertical volume slider, percentage and large +/− buttons.
TransportDockx 0, y 596 · 480 × 136Full-width 44 px mini track strip and 80 px transport row with 12 px top padding. No volume row.
Navigationx 0, y 732 · 480 × 68Playing, Queue, Browse, Search. Providers is reached from Browse; Players from the header.

Now Playing artwork remains 288 × 288 px, centred within the 400 px content pane. The playback timeline sits below track, artist and album/source information, with elapsed and total time flanking a horizontal seek slider. Its touch strip is 48 px tall. List rows are at least 72 px tall. Transport buttons are 64 × 56 px and the central button is 72 × 72 px.

The 80 px VolumeDock uses 56 × 56 px +/− buttons and a 48 px-wide slider touch strip containing a 236 px vertical rail. The reference slider occupies a 260 px-high interaction area; its percentage remains visible. Volume increases upwards. The dock ends at y=596, above the full-width TransportDock. The Search keyboard stays inside the 352 px usable content width with compact keys; validate typing comfort on hardware and consider a paged keyboard if required. Its results region scrolls independently. Device pixel density and finger testing may require larger targets.

Revision 1.1: this geometry supersedes the first firmware increment’s 480 × 468 content region and 200 px combined bottom dock at y=532. That firmware still uses bottom +/- volume buttons; implementing these two separate docks and the seek timeline is the next increment. The mockups specify the target, not the current firmware.

4. Interaction and state specification

Trigger / stateRequired behaviour
Centre transport buttonPlaying → send Stop. Stopped or paused → send Play. Label and icon describe the action: square / Stop or triangle / Play. Keep Pause distinct internally if existing integrations require it, without exposing a fourth control.
Stop and subsequent PlayDo not assume Stop resumes at the same position. Validate gateway/provider semantics. Preserve queue context where supported; document actual restart or resume behaviour during implementation.
Command pendingShow immediate pressed/pending feedback. Prevent duplicate submissions for the same action; reconcile with authoritative gateway state. On failure, show a short error and restore available controls.
Previous / NextUse existing gateway semantics. Disable actions unsupported by the selected player or source, including stations without skip support.
PlaybackTimeline / seekingUse the selected track’s authoritative position and duration. While dragging, preview the target time without gateway commands; send one seek on release. Reconcile with returned state. Reject a seek if track or player changed during the gesture. Disable seeking while pending, offline, unsupported, or duration is unknown; live radio shows “Live” without an active seek slider. Do not confuse this slider with volume.
VolumeDock sliderApply to the selected player. Higher means louder. Provide a 48 px-wide touch strip around the vertical rail; throttle drag updates and send a final value on release. During dragging show the tentative value distinctly; after release reconcile to reported volume. Reject a stale gesture if the player changes. Disable where unsupported or offline.
VolumeDock +/−Use the existing 5% boundary stepping: at 12%, + sends 15% and − sends 10%; at 15%, + sends 20% and − sends 10%. Clamp to 0–100%, disabling the appropriate button at each endpoint. Show authoritative feedback and prevent duplicate pending commands.
Tap player headerOpen Players and remember the previous view. Selecting a player subscribes to its state and returns to that view. Do not move music between rooms implicitly. Keep commands disabled while the switch is pending.
Tap source in BrowseOpen Providers. Selection returns to that source’s Browse view and scopes Search. Existing playback continues. Back restores the previous selection.
Queue rowTap to play that queue entry through the current integration. Highlight the authoritative active entry. Preserve stable entry identity across queue updates. Defer reorder and removal to explicit detail actions if supported.
SearchSearch the selected provider. Debounce input, cancel or ignore stale responses, and distinguish loading, no results and errors. Done hides the keyboard; an empty field shows recent searches only if implemented.
Long metadataReserve fixed heights. Wrap the main title to at most two lines; truncate list and dock labels with ellipsis. Avoid moving transport controls or continuous marquee animation.
Back navigationNested Browse, detail and selection views replace content and expose a 48 px Back target in the header. Bottom tabs return to primary views; preserve list position and search text.

Empty, radio and connection states

Idle: show a quiet artwork placeholder and “Choose something to play” with a Browse action. Both docks retain their geometry; transport is disabled until a valid command context exists. Radio: use station art and programme metadata when supplied, replace the seek timeline with “Live” for live streams and disable unsupported skips. Volume remains available when the player supports it. Missing artwork: show the built-in placeholder immediately.

Connecting or disconnected: retain last known metadata with a visible stale/offline label, disable network commands and offer Retry in the content area. An unavailable player remains visible but disabled. Missing source credentials or provider errors use a focused message and Back action. State is never communicated by colour alone.

6. Implementation approach

The agreed implementation direction uses native LVGL on the ESP32-S3 with ESP-IDF, retaining the Atlas/Music Assistant integration. These stack and hardware details come from the design conversation and should be checked against the repository before coding. This artefact does not establish the installed LVGL version or independently verify issue #15.

  1. Validate portrait hardware. Confirm panel rotation at 480 × 800, touch-coordinate transforms, corner accuracy, enclosure orientation and memory requirements. If native rotation is unavailable, measure software rotation before accepting it.
  2. Introduce tokens and shell. Build the header, content-plus-side-dock region, full-width TransportDock and navigation against simulated state. Match the geometry in this document before wiring screen-specific behaviour.
  3. Bind shared state and commands. Adapt existing player, track, queue, provider, volume and connectivity models. Distinguish playback state from command-pending state. Perform network work off the LVGL UI path and marshal results through the repository’s supported UI-thread mechanism.
  4. Compose the six screens. Reuse the shell and both docks; keep selection separate from playback actions. Implement capability-aware controls and all essential loading, empty and failure states.
  5. Add artwork safely. Resize at the gateway where existing infrastructure allows, bound decoding and caching, show placeholders immediately, and discard results belonging to an old track/player. Avoid fetching full-resolution artwork onto the device.
  6. Validate on hardware. Check touch ergonomics, playback feedback, provider behaviour, queue changes, keyboard space, memory and long-running responsiveness.

Memory and responsiveness

A single 480 × 800 RGB565 framebuffer is 768,000 bytes (~750 KiB); two are ~1.46 MiB. A 288 × 288 RGB565 artwork image is 165,888 bytes (~162 KiB), before decoder workspace and transfer buffers. The conversation assumes 8 MB PSRAM, which must be confirmed. Account separately for panel buffers, LVGL heap, images, fonts, networking and rotation buffers; do not infer usable heap from nominal PSRAM size.

Keep one active screen tree where practical; reuse both docks, use bounded list rendering and cache only a small number of resized images. Avoid blocking network calls and expensive effects in touch callbacks. HTML and LVGL share design tokens and component contracts, rather than rendering code. Use the project’s actual LVGL APIs once its version is known.

Appearance settings and staged navigation

The header has a 48 × 48 settings cog at x = 8, y = 8, followed by the screen title and output player selector. Settings provides Green, Blue, Red, Orange, Purple and Grey palettes, each in Dark and Light appearance: twelve themes. Use semantic roles for background, surface, card, active selection, primary text, secondary text and accent. Shared LVGL styles update existing controls and future list rows together without rebuilding screens or changing geometry. Persist palette and appearance in the controller NVS namespace; invalid palette values fall back to Green.

The bottom navigation is Playing, Queue, Browse and Search, in that order. During staged development, unfinished destinations remain visible and disabled. Players is accessed through the header selector. Music source selection is accessible from Settings until Browse is implemented. Both playback docks remain persistent. The narrow volume caption reads “Vol” to avoid truncation.

Acceptance captures all twelve themes on Playing, Settings, Players and Music Sources, checks fixed captions for truncation, verifies the four navigation destinations and disabled interactions, and checks geometry and containment in each theme. Hardware display and touch validation remains separate.

Gateway-served cover art

The Atlas gateway adds an opaque controller_art_id to current queue items and serves authenticated /device/artwork/{id} responses. Music Assistant resolves provider images; the gateway crops and resizes them to 288 × 288 and converts them into a fixed RGB565 packet. Missing, failed or unsupported cover art returns a neutral generic record cover. Real and generic cover pixels retain their original colours in every theme.

The ESP32 downloads on a separate task, validates the complete packet, keeps reusable image buffers in PSRAM and derives the 40 × 40 transport thumbnail locally. Before applying a response it verifies the player, queue, track and artwork identity. Network work does not hold LVGL locks. The theme-aware local placeholder remains visible while loading or if the gateway is unavailable. Acceptance mocks the gateway packet contract, tests stale and malformed results and captures both cover types in all twelve themes.

7. Acceptance checklist

  • All six views fit an actual 480 × 800 display, including the 80 px right VolumeDock, with no shell scrolling, clipped controls or distorted artwork.
  • TransportDock stays at y = 596 and VolumeDock at x = 400, y = 64 across primary, selection and keyboard states. Previous, Play/Stop and Next preserve their order.
  • Browse and Search keep working while both docks control the selected player. Volume remains visible even with the Search keyboard open.
  • Provider selection leaves the player unchanged; player selection follows that player’s queue and track without transferring playback.
  • Playing, stopped, paused, live radio, idle, unavailable, disconnected and command-failure states are usable and accurate.
  • Long track names, missing artwork, long lists and large search results stay within the narrower 400 px content pane. Timeline seeking sends once on release, ignores stale track/player gestures and disables for live or unsupported media. Volume slider and 5% buttons reconcile with authoritative state and clamp at endpoints.
  • Artwork/network updates do not block touch feedback. Establish a measured feedback target (proposed ≤100 ms for local visual response) and memory budget on the device.
  • Verify Play after Stop for the actual Sonos/gateway path, touch alignment at all four corners, volume capability and reconnect behaviour.

8. Scope and traceability

Design context: Ripper #577 remote Music Controller work and the supplied discussion of Music Controller issue #15. The attached collage establishes the current landscape UI; this document replaces its layout with the agreed portrait direction. No live repository or issue audit was performed for this consolidation.

Agreed: portrait target, three transport controls, persistent bottom transport and right-side volume docks, seek timeline on Now Playing, separate player/provider selection and a reusable native UI. Reference choices: compact track strip, four bottom destinations, the exact tokens and revised geometry above. VolumeDock is persistent on all screens for now; selectively hiding it while browsing is a future option. To validate: hardware rotation, installed framework versions, available providers, Stop/Play semantics and measured memory/performance. Group management, drag reordering, artwork palette extraction and dock-specific passive modes are future refinements.
